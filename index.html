    How cold is too cold?

    A dry, thick winter coat is excellent insulation. Horses with one don't start burning extra energy to stay warm until about 18°F (Rutgers, University of Minnesota). That's why the Unclipped setting rarely calls for a blanket above 30°F.

    Wet changes everything. A wet or thin coat loses most of that protection, closer to 59°F (Rutgers), which is why rain moves the call up.

    Most horses in mild climates never grow that coat, so they're blanketed like clipped horses. Shelter, plenty of hay and good body condition all help.

    General guidance from university extension services, not veterinary advice. Your vet knows Mikey best.
